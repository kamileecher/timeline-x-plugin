# Compose a graphic with the footage

Read this when adding a Motif over footage or choosing between a small graphic
and a full-frame beat. The target frame is the evidence for placement. Return
to the main skill for creation, guarded placement, in-place repair, and paged
verification.

## Inspect before authoring

Capture the target range at a representative settled moment and any important
gesture or composition change. Identify the subject, product, existing labels,
caption band, readable interface, and platform safe areas. Sketch the useful
remaining area and the size needed to understand the graphic. Do not assume
that a corner is empty throughout the range.

Choose the graphic form for the available space. A short label may fit beside
the subject; a relationship diagram may need a full-frame moment. A full-frame
beat is deliberate coverage of the footage, with a return point planned. An
overlay leaves the subject and essential action clear. Do not silently turn a
requested overlay into an opaque title card because the first layout is crowded.

Example: a speaker occupies the right half and captions fill the lower band.
A two-step explanation can use the left area if both lines remain readable at
delivery size. A six-step process cannot be solved by making the same panel
tiny; use a simpler sequence or an intentional full-frame diagram.

## Use a reference with a defined purpose

Inspect the actual selected image before claiming an adaptation. A contact
sheet may contain several independent designs; identify the single useful
unit. Borrow its hierarchy and proportions, then fit the current text and shot.
Keep composition reference, style reference, logo asset, and production footage
distinct. A reference person's position is not evidence about this shot, and
their picture does not need to become production media.

Retain Project style when borrowing a layout from another style. Add shadows,
panels, or effects only when they solve a demonstrated contrast or hierarchy
problem. An attractive standalone preview is still only a preview.

## Motion and contrast

Settle the important information while the associated speech or action is
current. Plan entrance, reading hold, and exit separately. Keep the readable
hold long enough for the actual text; test the longest approved version.
Use restrained motion when busy footage already supplies movement.

Keep the page background transparent for overlays. Internal panels may provide
contrast. A deliberate full-frame graphic can own its opaque surface within
the Motif; it still uses the same offline capture and Studio compositor. No
network background, browser capture renderer, or alternate visual pipeline is
introduced.

## Verify after placement

Inspect composed entrance, settled, middle, and exit frames at project-grid
times. Confirm that the graphic stays in frame, text fits, captions and subject
remain clear, and the occupied area is balanced. Check both bright and dark
source moments if the footage changes. Distinguish a transient animated state
from genuine overflow by sampling around the suspect time.

Repair placement or scale when the composition is wrong; repair content props
or the existing source when the design itself fails. Reuse the same Library
asset and Timeline layer. Repeat fresh verification after every mutation;
older frames no longer prove the current result. Export acceptance must use the
same composition and timing, and must be reported separately if no export was
requested or inspected.

# Outdoor, travel, and sports

Use this for packs, travel equipment, outdoor accessories, and sports products.
Pick one ordinary use scenario the product is designed for; spectacular scenery
does not show whether the product works.

Show preparation, use, and return to the product in a consistent location.
Keep load, straps, closures, body position, and weather continuous. Preserve
ambient sounds when they help establish the action. Choose music from the
film's tone rather than treating all outdoor footage as a silent field test.

Example concept for a travel pouch: place the supplied items beside it, pack
them, close the actual fastening, and put the pouch into the intended bag.
The viewer sees scale and access without an invented capacity statement.

Do not stage dangerous stunts, exaggerated loads, or simulated extreme-weather
tests as evidence. Generated motion cannot establish protection ratings,
endurance, charging speed, or fitness outcomes. Check the real product's shape,
equipment contact, and terrain/body continuity before placement. Keep verified
ratings and dimensions in editable text instead of generating them into pixels.

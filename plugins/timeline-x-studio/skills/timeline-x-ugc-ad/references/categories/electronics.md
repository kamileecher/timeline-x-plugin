# Electronics and audio

Use this for peripherals, cables, microphones, speakers, and similar devices.
Choose one task the supplied product actually supports. An unboxing establishes
identity and construction; it does not demonstrate a feature by itself.

- **Peripheral or accessory:** show the connection, the hand action, and the
  corresponding visible response. Keep ports, controls, cables, and product
  proportions consistent between shots.
- **Audio device:** use a real recorded comparison only when its conditions and
  source are known. Match listening levels and keep music out of the comparison.
  Generated sound cannot establish microphone quality or speaker performance.
- **Material detail:** a close-up can show texture, a hinge, or an actual button.
  Do not invent a hidden mechanism, destructive test, or waterproof rating.

Example concept for a supplied keyboard: a hand reaches across a cluttered
desk, the next shot shows the user's actual setup, then a close shot shows the
confirmed control in use. A factual overlay names the control rather than
claiming a productivity gain.

Inspect the input/action/result relationship and readable controls. Claims
about speed, range, compatibility, battery life, or sound require supplied facts;
the film's visual polish supplies none of those measurements.

# A product demonstration the viewer can understand

Read this before choosing a new UGC-ad concept. Use the category guide for the
specific action, then return to the main skill for tools, approval, placement,
and verification. A UGC look describes camera and delivery style; it does not
authorize a fabricated customer, testimonial, or personal experience.

## Choose a visible question

Write one sentence: "The viewer needs to see whether this product can ___ in
___." Fill it from confirmed product facts and the intended audience. A useful
hook opens that question; the demonstration answers it.

| Situation | Opening action | Demonstration | Closing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A repeated inconvenience | Show the task before the product enters | Repeat the task with the product | Point to the relevant confirmed feature |
| A material or texture matters | Start close enough to read the surface | Show handling, application, or movement | Name the material without inventing an outcome |
| A mechanism is unfamiliar | Show the finished result briefly | Reveal the actual operating steps | Invite the viewer to inspect the product details |
| A choice depends on fit | Show the use environment or scale | Show the product in that same setting | State the fit facts the user supplied |

Avoid exaggerated failure, fear, fake scarcity, and unsupported comparisons.
A direct, useful opening is enough. In Turkish copy, preserve natural spoken
phrasing and exact product terms rather than importing English sales slogans.

## Allocate time by information

Give the demonstration the longest useful span. Combine the problem and hook
when they show the same thing. Let the result settle long enough to be seen;
do not add a separate proof card that repeats it. Reserve a readable closing
for the real product name and the requested action. These are editorial jobs,
not a fixed five-shot template.

For each shot, specify the product angle, hand or body action, camera distance,
light, relevant sound, and the result visible at the end. Use a consistent
product, person, wardrobe, and setting across the plan. A motion prompt such as
"place the folded bag beside the open bag on the same table" is more reviewable
than "show amazing convenience".

## Feed placement and the close

In a feed placement, the product and its mark appear within the first two to
three seconds, on the product itself rather than as a floating logo, and the
brand returns at the close. When the opening shows the task before the product
enters, keep that lead-in within the same window. Give the one thing only this
product has, from the confirmed facts, one full-frame shot that holds at least
1.5 s. When identity matters, name the product on screen with a short label
kept up for at least 0.6 s plus 0.25 s per word.

Reserve the final 2.5–4 s for a calm closing card: the product name, a call to
action of no more than six words, and the store or handle, placed within the
safe areas of every requested aspect. Keep grain, film-print
curves, and similar texture effects off exact text unless the user asks for
them.

## Separate illustration from evidence

Actual recorded demonstrations may support only what their conditions show.
Generated footage can illustrate operation or an intended use; it cannot prove
durability, clinical results, measured performance, genuine taste, or a real
person's endorsement. Do not label a generated before/after as a measured result.
Use supplied reviews or test results only with correct wording and attribution.

Choose one sound role. Preserve mechanism, texture, pouring, or handling sounds
when they explain the action. Use narration when a short explanation is needed;
music is optional and must leave that explanation audible. A category is not a
mandatory genre or a reason to mute useful source sound.

Before approval, check that every claim has a source, every reference image
shows this product, and every exact word will remain editable in post. Before
delivery, check that a first-time viewer watching muted could name the product
and the requested action. After
generation, inspect identity, hands, action continuity, material, and scale
before placing the asset. Report missing visual or audio evidence explicitly.

# Food and beverage

Use this for edible products and drinks. Build around preparation, texture,
serving, or packaging facts; a fictional reaction cannot establish real taste.

Keep ingredients, container, portion, color, and preparation stages consistent.
Use a close view for pouring, breaking, stirring, or serving when that action
communicates the product. Preserve useful source sound; music and narration
must leave the action understandable.

Example concept for a supplied tea: show the actual package, the documented
preparation, then the serving vessel in the same setting. Any ingredient,
quantity, or preparation time shown in text comes from the verified label.

Do not invent nutrition, allergens, health benefits, weight effects, awards,
reviews, or reactions presented as customer testimony. Generated steam,
crunch, condensation, or consumption is a styling choice, not measured food
quality. Check hands, lips, vessel contact, and portion continuity before
placement. Keep readable label information in post when exactness matters.

# Time the promo from its real sound

Read this when narration, music, or product sound leads the approved promo.
It complements the existing creative and theme references; it does not replace
their storyboard gate or the Motion Graphics placement contract.

## Choose a pacing driver

| Driver | Choose when | Timing source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narration | The product needs explanation | Actual approved recording and valid phrase timing |
| Music | The product can be understood visually | An inspected music section and selected audible accents |
| Product sound | An action or interaction explains the product | The actual demo's sound/action relationship |

Existing recorded audio and Library music are valid starting points. Generate
new audio only within the user's request and existing approvals. Voice/model
selection belongs to the current voice or music workflow and settings; this
reference does not add an override or choose another provider.

## Narration-led execution

Write short phrases that describe confirmed outcomes or visible actions. Read
the proposed script aloud before approval. Replace broad adjectives with a
concrete action, without inventing speed, cost savings, or customer evidence.
Exact product name, feature labels, URL, and CTA remain editable text.

After approval, prepare the actual narration through the voiceover workflow.
Listen for pronunciation, delivery, and natural joins. Measure its real length
and valid phrase boundaries before fixing the visual ranges. Estimated reading
speed helps planning but is not a placement receipt or exact word timing.

Example plan for a confirmed calendar tool: show the crowded view while the
narration describes the problem, show the real action while it explains the
feature, then hold the resulting view and approved CTA. Keep illustrative
interface numbers clearly identified as samples. Do not promise fewer clicks
or time saved unless the supplied facts support that claim.

Map each visual to its spoken job and leave reading time where the narration
pauses. If actual narration exceeds the approved extent, revise the copy or
timing within the authorization instead of speeding speech blindly or hiding
the final phrase. A material storyboard change needs a new decision.

## Music-led execution

Choose and listen to the section that fits the film. Mark a few useful accents:
opening, product reveal, feature change, and closing. A tempo estimate or
generation prompt does not guarantee that a beat or drop exists at a requested
time. Use only advertised analysis capability if available; otherwise mark
the audible points by inspection and report the evidence level.

Leave each screen readable even when its entrance lands on an accent. Do not
change every beat just because the music supplies one. The closing needs a
settled CTA rather than an abrupt final impact that cuts the words off. Keep
important UI motion and demo sound intact when they are the actual proof.

## Resolve and verify the frame map

Write approved scene-plan beats with final Project-grid ranges, source assets,
copy, audio driver, and the entrance/settled/exit timing. Convert absolute audio
anchors to each Motif's local frame clock. Round once to the Project frame grid;
do not repeatedly mix milliseconds, frames, and microseconds. Follow the main
Motion Graphics workflow for batches and exact coverage.

Keep speech and music identifiable with appropriate audio roles. Use
`auto_duck_audio` when speech leads; listen after processing. Trim or arrange
music to the real content end through the music workflow so audio does not
extend the film accidentally.

Check the complete promo with sound and composed frames: spoken meaning agrees
with the visible feature, exact words remain readable, transitions fit the
chosen sound, and the first/last audio are intentional. A successful audio job
or graphic receipt alone does not establish the finished synchronization.

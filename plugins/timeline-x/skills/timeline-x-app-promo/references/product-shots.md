# Product shots by beat

Read this when building the storyboard of a product, launch, or software promo.
Pick one shot for each beat by the job the beat does, then build it from this
product's real interface, object, and numbers. The table names a grammar, not
a finished look. It adds no tool and authorizes no generation or edit.

| Beat's job | Shot | What happens | About |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Stacked chores | three to five short lines pile up in place, one verb lit in each, a tick lighting on the beat; needs no product footage | 3.5–5 s |
| Hook, physical goods | Hero object | the product turning under a raking edge light, once per film (3D Studio or a generated shot; layered stills for a flat object) | 4–6 s |
| The problem grows | Closing in | labelled tags on three depth layers drift toward the viewer, near ones large and sharp, far ones small and dim | 3.5–4.5 s |
| The problem in numbers | Drawn chart | bars or a line drawing from real data (the bar-compare Motif); only with real numbers | 3–4.5 s |
| The turn | Held line | the one-line position split across two beats, its second half set slightly softer, one accent word, a locked frame; the shortest shot | about 2 s |
| The input | Typing field | one wide field with a typing caret; pressing its button is the cut to the next shot | 2–3 s |
| The output | Results resolving | rows arrive as empty placeholders that fill with real content in the same boxes, a status chip per row turning to done | 3.5–4.5 s |
| The work | Two panes | one side acting, the other answering live; often the longest shot; one camera move at a time | 4–5 s |
| Operations | Tracker | a status card beside its evidence, steps completing one by one along a line (the event-timeline Motif) | 3–4 s |
| Developer proof | Terminal | rebuilt as a Motif, never screen-recorded: a typed command, its output, and a closing status line, timed to the narration | 3–5 s |
| Reach | Route map | a flat designed map with routes drawing on (the route-map Motif), never live map tiles | 3–4.5 s |
| Proof, a number | Count-up | figures of fixed width so nothing shifts as it counts, a small label, one honest bar or ring; only an approved figure | about 3 s per figure |
| Proof, a person | Testimonial | a single quoted sentence, followed by the person's face, name, role, and company mark; the customer's own consented photo, never a stock face | 3.5–4.5 s |
| An objection | Comparison | a table of five to eight rows in the buyer's words; competitors described by their category rather than named; where coverage is partial, show it as partial, since a column of all dashes reads as a straw man | 4.5–5.5 s |
| The close | End card | the mark, one line, one call to action, the address; stays on screen for 2.5 s or more and rhymes with the opening | 3–4 s |

## Put the shots together

- Adjacent shots never share a camera move; alternate locked frames with
  moving ones.
- Alternate busy and calm: a title, a number, or a quote sits between two
  dense product shots. A 45-second film runs roughly three busy shots to four
  calm ones, plus the type frames at each end.
- Change the ground colour, or light against dark, only at the story's turns
  (problem to product to proof), never inside a section.
- Use the real product only: the real interface, or a recreation labelled as
  illustrative. Never invent a feature, a screen, or a number.

## Software as a lit object

For software, treat the screen as a product on a set, not as a flat
recording.

- Write the four to seven on-screen actions in order and the single moment
  where the product clearly does its impressive thing. Demo content must look real:
  plausible names, numbers, and text, never sparse grey placeholders.
- Place a capture sharp enough for the shot's magnification on one plane,
  tilted back and turned so lines of text run diagonally across the frame;
  `set_corner_pin` on the capture layer gives that plane. Flip the diagonal at
  every cut and keep a good part of the frame near black.
- Most of the film lives in tight framings of a single control or a handful
  of rows; looser framings serve longer processes. Keep the one straight-on
  view of the entire window for the big moment, and go tight again right
  after it.
- Let length follow the action: processes such as typing or an answer
  streaming in run 5–9 s, accents such as a tab or a press 2–3.5 s, in
  alternation. The action starts within half a second of the cut, the result
  holds half a second to a second, and you cut before the camera's drift has
  come to rest.
- The interface acts on its own, with no pointer travelling between targets:
  a highlight slides over, buttons visibly depress, and text appears at around
  twelve characters per second. Join interface shots with hard cuts.
- End quietly: one line of copy, then the still mark held 2.5–4 s, and keep
  the big moment free of shakes and flashes.
- Give every visible action a close, small sound over near silence or very low
  music.
- Check a still at the middle of each shot: a single sharp subject the eye
  lands on within half a second, copy that still reads at half size, and
  neighbouring shots that change framing size or tilt direction.
